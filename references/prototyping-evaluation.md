### Procedure

1. State which uncertainty the prototype or evaluation must resolve and what decision follows.
2. Choose the least elaborate artifact that preserves the interaction or context needed to answer the question.
3. Use representative content and states, including meaningful errors, permissions, interruptions, and data extremes.
4. Prepare neutral tasks and explicit completion criteria. Pilot logistics and known prototype limitations.
5. Conduct the appropriate expert or user evaluation; do not label an expert opinion as user evidence.
6. Analyze observed task behavior alongside stated perceptions. A visual preference alone does not establish better usability.
7. Prioritize remedies by impact, risk, evidence, and effort, then re-evaluate changes that affect the decision.
8. Communicate limitations and what a running implementation or further study still needs to establish.

### Outputs

Prototype strategy; study-ready artifact; evaluation protocol; observations; prioritized findings; remediation and retest decisions.

### Quality checks

Fidelity fits the question; participants and tasks fit the audience; synthetic content is labeled; evidence supports conclusions; methods and limitations are recorded; validation reaches the actual risky states.

### Limits and common failures

A prototype can conceal backend failure, timing, semantic, and hardware limitations. Preference testing cannot replace task observation. Benchmark comparisons require comparable conditions. Avoid promising statistical confidence from an unplanned convenience sample.
