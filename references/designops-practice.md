### Procedure

1. Identify the operational problem, affected teams, current process, outcomes, and decision ownership.
2. Map the flow of work and information, including discovery, implementation, support, maintenance, and system contributions.
3. Choose a proportionate operating model and accessible collaboration practices.
4. Set useful quality criteria and required evidence for the work's consequence and complexity.
5. Make assets and research discoverable with appropriate access, retention, provenance, and maintenance.
6. Assess health using rework, task quality, consistency, accessibility, learning, and operational burden alongside flow measures.
7. Coach skills using real evidence and practice rather than output volume alone.
8. Improve the library from observed failures and changed context; retire overlap or obsolete material.

### Outputs

Operating model; ownership/capacity map; facilitation plans; decision/critique record; governance and learning plan; maintenance backlog.

### Quality checks

Decisions have owners; evidence is accessible to the appropriate people; reviews are proportionate; maintenance has capacity; impact claims are supported; changes address observed problems.

### Limits and common failures

More documents, components, workshops, or screens do not automatically mean better design. Frameworks and rituals should solve a coordination or decision problem. A portfolio must not invent user quotes, metrics, role ownership, or outcomes.
