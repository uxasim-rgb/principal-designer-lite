### Apply a principle

1. State the actual decision, task, audience, context and affected behavior.
2. Classify the principle: requirement, performance model, psychological finding, construct, heuristic, maxim or project convention.
3. Retrieve the original source when a formal or empirical claim is material. Check population, task, measures and limitations.
4. Compare relevant contrary evidence and alternative explanations.
5. Translate the principle into a testable design hypothesis or scoped requirement.
6. Evaluate the actual task; keep the conclusion within the evidence.

### Important corrections

- Do not impose seven navigation items from Miller's historical memory work. Meaningful chunking and external memory are useful; menu limits need task evidence.
- Do not assume choice reduction always improves outcomes. The inspected meta-analysis record reports substantial contextual variation (S36).
- Do not apply a single responsiveness threshold to every task (S42).
- Do not turn a 4px or 8px spacing convention into a universal UX law (S01 to S03).
- Do not interpret general rules as domain safety or regulatory certification.
- S59 to S61 are a practitioner's index, not original experimental support for every concept. S67 to S69 have limited access; the original-source-review entries are research topics, not verified empirical claims.
