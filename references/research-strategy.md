# Research strategy, evidence, and planning

Operational guide and topic inventory. Procedures and checks are an original synthesis; use the source register for the authority, inspected portion, and limits of specific references.

### Procedure

1. Begin with the decision and consequences of getting it wrong. Convert assumptions into answerable questions and rank the uncertainty.
2. Review existing evidence before recruiting. Check source definitions, recency, population, context, and whether the evidence answers this decision.
3. Choose methods that can answer the question. Use behavioral evidence for actual task behavior and attitudinal methods for perceptions or stated needs; combine appropriately.
4. Specify audiences, sampling rationale, accessibility accommodations, recruitment risks, and feasible study logistics. Avoid universal sample-size recipes.
5. Prepare neutral questions or representative tasks. Pilot the protocol, prototype, recording arrangement, and task success definitions.
6. Conduct only authorized real sessions. If sessions cannot be conducted, deliver the protocol and assumptions; never manufacture quotes, participants, observations, saturation, or percentages.
7. Separate observations from explanations. Seek conflicting evidence, preserve source links, and explain what the study cannot establish.
8. Communicate findings in terms of decisions, consequences, confidence, limitations, and the next action. Assign owners and retain records according to the agreed policy.

### Outputs

Decision/research-question map; method and sampling rationale; accessible research plan; protocol; evidence-linked findings; recommendation and uncertainty register.

### Quality checks

The method fits the question; the population and context are stated; raw evidence supports claims; hypothetical examples are labeled; observations and assumptions are distinct; sample limits are visible; no causal or quantitative claim exceeds the design.

### Limits and common failures

Do not treat five interviews as a universal sample size, qualitative theme frequency as population prevalence, preference as task success, or a satisfaction score as a diagnosis. Preserve instrument wording, scoring, permissions, and context. NASA-TLX concerns perceived workload across several dimensions; verify the intended administration and interpretation.
