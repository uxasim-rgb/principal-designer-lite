# Example: research plan when there is no data yet

**Request:** "Our onboarding has a big drop-off. My PM thinks it's because the sign-up form is too long. Plan how to find out why." Only a screenshot of the form is supplied.

**Good response (abridged):**

**Decision this supports:** whether to shorten the sign-up form, change something else in onboarding, or both.

**What we know vs assume**
- Known: the form exists (screenshot); the team reports a "big drop-off".
- Not yet known: where in onboarding people leave, how many, which segments. "Big" needs a number from analytics.
- Hypothesis (PM): form length causes abandonment. Competing hypotheses: unclear value before sign-up, a required field people can't answer (company size, phone), email verification friction, technical errors on some devices.

**Plan**
1. **Check the funnel first (1 to 2 days).** Step-by-step completion from landing to activation, split by device and traffic source. Confirm the events fire correctly before trusting them. This tells us *where* people leave.
2. **Session replays of abandoners (if consented and masked).** 20 to 30 sessions at the main drop-off step, coded for what happened just before leaving.
3. **Five to eight moderated usability sessions** with people matching the target sign-up audience who have not used the product. Tasks: "Sign up and get to the point where you could [core job]." Observe hesitation, errors and comments; probe neutrally.
4. **A one-question exit prompt** at the drop-off step ("What stopped you today?") if traffic allows, treated as directional, not representative.

**What this can and can't tell us:** sessions show why individuals struggle; they don't give a population rate. The funnel gives rates but not reasons. Together they let us pick a change, which we then validate with a controlled test or before-and-after comparison with the same event definitions.

**Deliverables after fieldwork:** funnel with drop-off by step, a findings list linked to session evidence, and a recommendation.

**Why this is a good example:**
- No invented findings, percentages or quotes. Future tense throughout.
- Treats the PM's theory as one hypothesis among several.
- Starts with cheap existing evidence before recruiting.
- States what each method can't establish.
